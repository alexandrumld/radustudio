# Radu Studio — research and creative decision

Research date: 15 October 2026. Read the actual live pages and rendered desktop/mobile versions where available. This is design research, not independent verification of other studios' claims. Screenshots and captured page text are in `/workspace/research-captures/`.

## Decision

**Online Architecture: places on the internet, designed with intent.** Retain the holding page's strongest ownable thought, but make it an expressive composition system rather than a visual pun about buildings. Radu designs and builds websites that give a business a credible, distinctive place online. The interface itself demonstrates the union of design and engineering.

Choose Direction A below, softened by Direction C's editorial warmth. The identity should be recognizable when stripped of animation: a substantial wordmark, a modular geometric R mark, disciplined annotations, a controlled warm palette and unusually deliberate type composition. No borrowed assets, reference layouts or signature interactions.

## What is verified about Radu

- [radu.studio](https://radu.studio) currently serves a minimal holding page: “Radu Studio / Online Architecture / Coming Soon.” It provides no verifiable client evidence or approved public prices.
- The mission establishes founder-led, Oulu-based studio status and `team@radu.studio`. The website should use those facts, without expanding them into claims about a large team, a legal entity, years of experience, awards or results.
- The prior €1,250 / €3,200 / monthly-care numbers are proposals, not approved public pricing. Keep them out of public copy unless the recovery of existing materials establishes approval.
- A demonstration must be called a demonstration in the work index **and** its detail view. A made-up project should never look like an undisclosed paying client. Present craft and reasoning, not invented outcomes.

## References and useful principles

### 1. Studio Freight — experimental editorial systems

[Homepage](https://www.studiofreight.com/) · [Info](https://www.studiofreight.com/info) · [Contact](https://www.studiofreight.com/contact)

Actually observed: homepage DOM, Info and Contact text; rendered 1440×1000 and 390×844 homepage screenshots. The homepage surrounds a modest central statement with a disciplined atlas of varied work. It recomposes the imagery for mobile instead of shrinking the desktop canvas. A restrained serif-and-small-type system lets the work own the color. Info expands capabilities and principles; Contact includes an enquiry form, direct inquiry routes and personality through recommendations.

**Useful for Radu:** composition and restraint can communicate confidence more strongly than effects. Separate clear navigation and enquiry from the expressive work presentation. A tiny, consistent annotation system can unify very different case imagery.

**Do not transfer:** the collage layout, heraldic mark, signature language or portfolio density. Freight has evidence and reputation Radu cannot borrow. A cryptic mission line alone would leave a Finnish business owner unsure what to buy.

### 2. Basement — strong own voice with tangible capabilities

[Homepage](https://basement.studio/) · [Services](https://basement.studio/services)

Actually observed: live HTML copy, real project names, capability organization, direct email, navigation and lab positioning. A browser capture was attempted but screenshotting timed out; visual and motion quality were **not** validated in this session.

The headline states a confident attitude; the following paragraph names the combination of creativity, design and technology. Projects describe the commission and the studio's work. Capabilities are grouped around practical outputs, from websites to visual identity and marketing execution. A lab separates experimental work from commissioned work.

**Useful for Radu:** let distinctive tone coexist with concrete service nouns. Show how a project was solved. Clearly separate commissioned work from experiments. Make one original interactive idea part of the studio's craft.

**Do not transfer:** their profanity, “powerhouse” scale, big-client credibility or performance claims. Radu's stronger small-studio advantage is direct contact with the person making the work.

### 3. Lusion — specialization and capability proof

[Homepage](https://lusion.co/)

Actually observed: live HTML and rendered DOM. Its content explicitly specializes in 3D visual storytelling and interactive web experiences. Work labels distinguish concepts from other work. Projects carry discipline tags; contact offers general and new-business emails. The captured visual remained on a loading screen, so the completed visual experience was **not** assessed.

**Useful for Radu:** specificity makes experimental work commercially understandable. Truthful “concept” labels do not destroy a premium presentation. An original working interaction can prove capability without borrowed client logos.

**Do not transfer:** a heavy loading gate or a promise that every website needs immersion. The local service-business visitor must see a real proposition and enquiry path before any graphics finish loading.

### 4. Refokus — client situation before agency self-description

[Homepage](https://www.refokus.com/)

Actually observed: live HTML, rendered DOM and desktop/mobile screenshots. The visual capture remained at its loading counter; no claim is made about the completed motion experience. The strongest copy describes a recognizable business situation: a company's brand and website have fallen behind its product and ambition. Its case descriptions explain what complexity was made understandable, and list services and location.

**Useful for Radu:** describe the visitor's situation in plain language: “Your business has moved forward. Has your website?” Then tell them exactly what Radu delivers. Case studies should pair an attractive result with context, choices and scope.

**Do not transfer:** tech-startup-only positioning, “refokus” wordplay, their visual identity, awards or a large motion prerequisite. Radu needs broad industry flexibility.

### 5. KOTA — commercial questions are part of the design

[Homepage](https://kota.co.uk/) · [Contact](https://kota.co.uk/contact/)

Actually observed: full homepage copy and rendered desktop/mobile opening screens. Oversized expressive typography carries a direct creative point of view. The site then becomes explicit about services, framework, work, support and practical FAQs. Public pricing and project timing are presented as questions clients actually ask. The mobile capture showed a consent panel occupying much of the lower screen and service explanation below the dominant headline.

**Useful for Radu:** answer scope, process, feedback, support and how pricing is agreed. A service table can state the business need, deliverables and what happens next. Use one clear direct “Start a project” path.

**Do not transfer:** gradient hero, provocative headline, large-agency price ranges, performance statistics or unnecessary consent UI. Radu can communicate the actual service earlier and keep privacy simpler by omitting tracking.

### 6. Dunclyde — smaller-studio warmth and a narrow offer

[Homepage](https://www.dunclyde.com/)

Actually observed: live homepage text, About page text, and rendered desktop/mobile screenshots of both. The About page identifies its two founders by name. The page says it does only two things: impactful brands and interactive websites. Project captions are short and specific: brand refresh, art direction, website, and so on. It uses an approachable voice and a story-style enquiry form while retaining direct email and quick contact. It also visibly separates personal and unpublished experiments in a proposed sandbox. Homepage availability copy is undated (“September”), while news items shown in the captured text are older; that is a maintenance warning rather than a pattern to copy.

**Useful for Radu:** a small studio can be confidently specific. Human tone, narrow scope and clarity of responsibility are credible without pretending to be a large agency. Keep a direct email visible beside any custom form.

**Do not transfer:** lengthy sentence-building form controls, rotating color identity or stale availability claims. Fewer fields and a stable visual signature will serve Radu better. This session does not independently verify Dunclyde's revenue or business success.

### Additional local context

[WTF Design, Oulu/Kokkola](https://www.wtfdesign.fi/): read the actual homepage and rendered its desktop/mobile opening screens. It foregrounds partnership, a broad service range, named local projects and direct named sales contacts. Its self-description positions it as one of the north's larger agencies. Radu should compete through unusually focused web craft and founder access, rather than by imitating full-service agency scale. Finnish localization could help locally, but only reviewed Finnish copy should be published.

## Three competing directions

| | A — Online Architecture | B — Small Studio, Sharp Edges | C — A Contemporary Creative House |
|---|---|---|---|
| Central idea | Design a distinctive place for a business online; every part has a job | Direct founder access and independent taste are the advantage | A studio as an editorial house: every project gets a carefully composed world |
| Visual identity | Modular, engineered R/space mark; heavy wordmark; structured but asymmetric type | Huge compact wordmark, assertive statements, underlines, candid marginal notes | Rich serif/sans tension, oversized folios, full-bleed photography, quiet captions |
| Palette | Mineral paper, near-black, one signal orange/red | Dark burgundy, warm yellow, off-white | Bone, deep green or blue, muted silver, one warm accent |
| Composition | Wide typographic openings, suspended modules, contrasting dense and open areas | Typographic posters interrupted by work, intentionally abrupt rhythm | Editorial spreads, generous space, highly controlled image crops |
| Motion | Elements align, separate and recombine; 180–650 ms, precise easing | Snappy cuts, hard changes, quick typographic reveals | Patient wipes, graceful image crops and restrained transitions |
| Memorable interaction | A keyboard/touch control recomposes the modular identity through a few designed states | A brief-builder that answers “what do you actually need?” with useful plain-language guidance | A curated work viewer with alternate detail and full-image modes |
| Commercial merit | Connects design and development; credible across industries; feels deliberate | Human and confident; makes a new studio feel accessible | High perceived care and taste; strong for culture, hospitality, premium brands |
| Main risk | Can drift into sterile grids or architecture cliché | Can become performatively rude or make conservative clients feel unwelcome | Can become a generic fashionable luxury/editorial portfolio |
| How to guard it | Bold original silhouette, warm type, plain service wording, no literal blueprints | Understate the wit; don't make the buyer the joke | Bespoke identity mark and strong business-context copy |
| Fit | Strongest foundation: ownable existing thought and direct design/build relevance | Useful voice ingredient, weaker master identity for both audiences | Useful art-direction warmth, weaker differentiation alone |

**Recommendation:** A with C's editorial care and a small amount of B's human candor. This is a synthesis of principles, not a blend of reference layouts. Distinctiveness should come from a reusable Radu composition rule: one module can become many useful arrangements, just as a business's website can be authored around its needs.

## Implementation implications

- Keep “Online Architecture” as the brand thought; directly alongside it say **“Distinctive websites. Designed and built for your business.”** The first viewport must also say founder-led, Oulu/Finland, and provide Work / Start a project.
- Create a real responsive graphic using SVG or CSS. The signature control can cycle three predetermined compositions. Use native `<button>`, visible focus, a simple accessible name and reduced-motion support. Keep content and navigation stationary. Interaction should take a second, not become a task.
- Use a tight font system: one expressive display family, one very readable text face; mono annotations only when they add hierarchy. Self-host correctly licensed fonts. Composition must work before fonts load.
- Selected work should be honest and specific. Two deeply presented demonstrations are more useful than six fictional client cards. Each needs a visible status, the intended business scenario, scope and design choices, several real captures, and “What this demonstrates” rather than results claims.
- Service content: design and structure / development and launch / website care. State deliverables and how feedback works. Do not offer every agency discipline to appear bigger.
- Process: understand → design → build → launch and care. Each step should answer what Radu does and what the client needs to provide.
- Studio copy should explain the benefit of founder-led work: fewer handovers, decisions with the maker, a coherent design/build process. Do not imply guaranteed response times or dedicated account staff.
- Enquiry: name, email, brief; optional budget and timing if useful. Explain what the click does. If only an email draft is available, say **“Prepare email”**, then explicitly say it opens the user's email app and that they still need to send it. Keep `team@radu.studio` copyable. Never simulate successful delivery.
- No forced loaders, scroll hijacking, hidden contact information, autoplay audio or cursor-dependent navigation. Readable HTML is available immediately. Allow graphic and motion enhancement to arrive later.
- English first. Add verified Finnish as a real later editorial task, not machine copy presented as reviewed localization.

## What to challenge in refinement

1. **Creative:** Does the brand remain recognizable with the projects removed? Does the signature graphic look intentional in a still image? Does every large headline have meaningful composition, not merely size?
2. **Commercial:** Can a mobile visitor identify web design/development, studio location, work status and next step in ten seconds? Does every case explain who it is for? Are any words implying evidence we do not have?
3. **Craft:** Are headline wraps designed at 390, 768, 1024 and 1440? Can all work be read without hover? Does the enquiry work with only the keyboard? Is motion optional? Does anything disappear when JS is disabled or graphics are slow?

No numerical self-rating is useful here. The evidence should be rendered screens, completed user journeys and fixes to the weakest sections.
